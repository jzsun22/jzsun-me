JustGrow: an AI-empowered parenting companion

One app for the whole job of early parenting: tracking, memories, and family planning.

Problem

Early parenting is split across apps, and each one adds to the mental load

Tracking in one app, photos in another, updates in group chats. Each is one more thing to keep in sync, for parents who are already stretched thin.

I joined when JustGrow was just an idea. The founder owned direction; I owned UX, the Flutter frontend, parts of the backend, and the agent prompts.

Challenge

Parents had no time to learn a new app, so switching had to cost nothing

JustGrow asked them to move tracking, photos, and family planning into one app. To be worth switching, it had to be as convenient as the apps they already used, with smoother flows and fewer taps. Every feature, and the screen that came with it, had to earn its place.

Solution

One chat to log everything, and a relay race to hand off care

Parents log a feed or a nap in plain language, and AI agents turn it into events. Gleam Calendar lists those events and their durations, so parents and caregivers can see the day at a glance.

Screens on this page are rebuilt in HTML with JustGrow’s design-system tokens, so retired, shipped, and proposed designs read as one set. Some are interactive; try them.

Those same events power the handoff. The founder wanted passing care between caregivers to be easy, and invisible work to be visible. I designed the handoff as a relay race: pick the children and your partner, hand over, and the receiver reviews recent events before accepting. I built it end to end, including edge cases such as two simultaneous handoffs and handoffs that expire before anyone accepts them.

Launch

We launched a fully functional tracker, in English and Chinese

The launch included tracking with the chat agent built in, growth charts to visualize the data, and the care handoff. We checked the localization by hand instead of relying on machine translation, so both languages use the words parents actually say. For grandparents abroad, times always show in the baby’s timezone.

Iteration

I designed a replacement that asks nothing

In an App Store review, a night nurse called the handoff “perfect for overnight care.” But analytics showed few families used it. Every handoff needed both caregivers to remember a step: one to hand over, the other to accept. I had designed for an idealized parent whose only concerns were the baby and the app. Real parents are juggling far more.

A better entry point would have repeated the same mistake, just cheaper. Every tracking event already recorded who did it. The app didn’t need parents to declare a handoff; it could read what they were already logging.

I proposed a replacement that lives in chat. Credit comes from existing events, and a weekly digest shows the family total plus a private count of your own. No new screens, no new agents. The founder retired the handoff; I handed off the replacement plan before I left, so I didn’t get to build it myself.

Beyond tracking

I consolidated: one place for memories, one design system

Photo sharing, from uploading to browsing, became Memory: one timeline where the family posts photos, videos, and notes. With privacy settings, it doubles as a private journal. I built its media pipeline end to end: storage on Google Cloud Storage, upload and access permissions, thumbnail generation, and caching.

In addition, tokens, type, and components now live in one system, with a log of deliberate exceptions so no one “fixes” them later.

Impact

200+ signups in nine months, with no paid marketing

Takeaway

Wanting an outcome isn’t the same as being willing to pay for it

Parents wanted to share the load, but the handoff asked for their attention at the worst moment. Now I weigh what a design asks of people before I scope it.
